Chunking is the process of splitting long documents into smaller pieces before embedding them. Embedding a whole document blurs many topics into one vector, which hurts retrieval.

Fixed-size chunking cuts text every N characters or tokens. It is simple but often splits sentences in half. Adding an overlap of 10 to 20 percent between chunks reduces lost context at the boundaries.

Paragraph chunking splits on blank lines ("\n\n"). It respects the author's own structure and usually keeps one idea per chunk, but paragraph lengths can vary a lot.

Sentence-based and recursive chunking are middle-ground options. Recursive splitters try paragraphs first, then sentences, then words until each chunk fits under a size limit.

In the chunk comparison experiment from module 07, paragraph chunks gave the most readable results, while small fixed chunks with overlap scored slightly better on exact-fact lookups.
